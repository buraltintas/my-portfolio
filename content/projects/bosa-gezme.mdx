<h2>Overview</h2>
<p>Boşa Gezme! is a community review site for physical home and living stores in Türkiye: furniture, beds, carpets, curtains, home textiles, lighting, kitchenware and appliances. You describe what you need in your own words, see the nearest stores first, and read reviews from people who actually went there. The name is Turkish for roughly "don't make the trip for nothing". Its tagline is "Bize Sor." ("Ask us."), and the home page opens with "Real stores. Real experiences."</p>
<p>The site sells nothing; there is no basket, no ordering and no payment. It runs on the web at <a href="https://bosagezme.com" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">bosagezme.com</a>.</p>

<h2>Problem</h2>
<p>Online search is good at "what can I buy?" It says much less about a physical store: whether the thing you want is on the floor, whether the staff know the products, how checkout and returns go. Usually you find out by going, and sometimes the trip is wasted.</p>

<h2>Solution</h2>
<p>Boşa Gezme! covers home and living retail only. You type a sentence such as "salonum için perde arıyorum" (I'm looking for curtains for my living room), and the results are ordered from nearest to farthest. Before you can review a store, the site confirms that you are at it, and the review scores the visit itself on eight criteria. The loop has four steps: discover, visit, review, and help the next person choose.</p>

<h2>Key Features</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Search in your own words, nearest first. Stores in your city that the community has already reviewed come up front.</li>
  <li>A review can be written only after the site confirms you are at the store.</li>
  <li>Eight criteria: availability, value, layout, staff care, staff knowledge, checkout, returns and cleanliness. A score of one or two needs a written reason.</li>
  <li>A chart of each store's score over time.</li>
  <li>Contributor levels from New Explorer up to Master Guide, which takes 100 reviews. A level brings no discount or perk; it is a community marker on your profile.</li>
  <li>Nationwide pages for each trade, city, district and chain. The furniture page, for example, covers 8,097 furniture stores in 81 provinces.</li>
  <li>An Assistant at /yardim that answers questions about how the site works, using only the texts the site publishes.</li>
  <li>Anonymous browsing, and sign-in with Google or an email code.</li>
  <li>Turkish, English, German and Russian. Reviews stay in the language they were written in and are never machine-translated.</li>
  <li>Paid placement, if there is any, is always labelled where you see it.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Technical Highlights</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Browser &rarr; Next.js BFF &rarr; Go API. Next.js 16 and React 19 render the pages on the server and act as the BFF, so the browser never talks to the API directly.</li>
  <li>A Go API (chi, pgx) on Google Cloud Run.</li>
  <li>PostgreSQL with PostGIS on Neon for distance search and visit checks. Neon suspends after five quiet minutes, so the API holds the whole live catalogue in memory, and crawlers reading store and city pages do not wake the database.</li>
  <li>Store records come from three places: the chains' own store locators, the showroom directories that furniture centres such as Masko, Modoko and Mobiliyum publish, and OpenStreetMap through Overpass, credited under ODbL. Scores come only from the site's own reviews and are never mixed with outside data.</li>
  <li>OpenAI is used in three places: as a fallback when the catalogue cannot answer a search on its own, in the Assistant, and in review moderation, where a word list catches insults before the model sees them.</li>
  <li>Sign-in codes go out through the Gmail API, images live in Cloud Storage, and secrets in Secret Manager.</li>
  <li>Changed pages are sent to search engines through IndexNow. OpenTelemetry and Prometheus cover tracing and metrics.</li>
</ul>

<h2>Why It Matters</h2>
<p>A wasted trip across the city costs an afternoon. Boşa Gezme! puts the experience of people who went to a store on that store's page and keeps every source apart: the address and location come from the brand or the map, and the score and the words come from the person who visited.</p>
